# Prediction - groundwork for the final goal (2026-09-13)

**Goal:** predict where the best farms are - and what they will read in game - from structure, instead of
searching every spot. The exact phase search proves what a spot holds; prediction decides which spots
(and which families) deserve it, and eventually could name record seeds directly.

## 1. What is already exact (the target is well defined)

- **In-game count from the layout.** `afk_rescan` (feet at y -39 over the -64 floor: `24^2 <= a^2 + b^2 + 24^2 <= 128^2`,
  no inner circle, outer radius 125.7, AFK point free within +-48 blocks) reproduces `/perimeterinfo`:
  full floor 49,669 = 49,669; farms 69.39 (18,598), 70.82 (18,261), 69.30, 68.28, 66.04 exactly; 70.64 +20;
  misses: 70.34 and 69.09 (~200 - unexplained, the the in-game numbers win). **So the target is "the best -39 count
  of a spot", computable from any layout.**
- **Layout from the seed, per class.** Phase model (docs\PHASE_MODEL.txt): for family f (bits 0-19) and class c
  (bits 20-34), all 8,192 top values give layouts fixed by 222 numbers B(0); a chunk is slime when
  w = phase (mod 5) (phase + 2 after the wrap). The best layout of a class costs 222 numbers, not 8,192 seeds.

## 3. The structure to exploit (theory)

B(0) of chunk i is the LCG output of `((f + P_i) ^ C) ^ M` with `P_i = x^2 a + x b + z^2 c + z d` (int32 pieces).
- **Without the xor, the phase differences between chunks would not depend on the seed at all** - a spot's
  quality would be a pure property of its P-differences. The xor with C (8 of the 15 class bits flipped,
  C >> 20 = 0x5E43) makes richness depend on (f, c) through carries. So richness = (spot geometry in
  P-space) x (carry pattern of f + P against C).
- **Transport hypothesis (H1):** two spots whose chunk offsets differ by a near-constant Delta in the low bits
  share rich families, shifted: f' = f - Delta (mod 2^20). Along one X column the x-part of P is the same, so
  lines with similar z-differences would share rich families - a candidate explanation for strong X columns.
- **Low-bit hypothesis (H3):** the farm is set by roughly the lowest 26 seed bits (family + class mod 64); the
  class's upper bits and v mostly rotate phases. If universal, a first pass over (family, class mod 64) -
  2^26 instead of 2^35 - could rank families ~500x cheaper (needs a sound bound to stay exact).

- **SISTER COLUMNS (found 2026-09-13): hot X columns repeat every 65,536 chunks (2^20 = 1,048,576 blocks).** Why: whether a
  chunk can be slime depends on the low 18 bits of (seed + P_i); inside a window only the DIFFERENCES P_i - P_c matter
  (a constant is absorbed by the family), and their low 18 bits are unchanged when cx moves by 2^16 (the x^2 term's
  2*cx*dx*0x4c1906 carries a factor 4; the x term cancels). In Z the same holds every 2^17 chunks. Data: the 69,955 hot
  X peaks use 8,702 of 65,536 residues (period exactly 2^16), and 30% of a hot column's sisters are hot (16x chance;
  shifted controls 0%). Each great spot has up to ~56 sister spots (same line, X + k * 1,048,576 blocks) with the same
  possible-chunk structure - with its rich family SHIFTED by the offset difference. Their FARMS are NOT as good
  (tested 2026-09-13, section 5.2): richness needs the high bits, which the shift scrambles.

## 6. What makes a family rich (study 2026-09-13, main\theory\research\phase_study.cpp)

- **Richness is a jackpot, not a gradient.** At the 70.34's AFK chunk, 2,001 kept families (top 5%): the rich family 0x0FFDB
  reaches 75 chunks; every other one 42-60 (median ~49). Possible-count rank of the jackpot family: #141.
- **A class sample finds a jackpot only if it hits the family's good classes.** Same position: best over 64 classes (class
  bits 6-14 = 0) = 74, over 4,096 = 75, over 512 random = 68 - all rank it #1 of 2,001. For ordinary families a random
  512-class sample ranks best (Spearman 0.87 vs the full truth; 64 structured classes 0.59; possible count 0.39).
- **As a spot predictor the cheap version is not enough.** 64-class pre-pass over all 15 positions of 30 searched spots:
  corr with the spot's true best count 0.43 (heat 0.23), with best cq 0.39 (heat 0.14). It ranks the 70.34 spot #1 but scores
  the other record spots mid-pack (61-65 while their truth is 72-76): their rich families have NO good class below 64.
- **The good classes of a rich family form a lattice at family-specific high bits.** 70.82's rich family 0x0A8BB: class >> 6 in
  {221, 229, 347, 349, 357, 365}; 69.30's 0x9A563: {265, 273, 393, 401} - steps of 8 and 128 (class bits 9 and 13 flip freely),
  on top of the low-bit window. Where the lattice sits differs per family, so no fixed cheap sample covers all jackpots; a
  sample big enough to (4,096 classes) costs 1/8 of the exact search.
- **Open question (D1, sharpened):** which class bits are "free" for a family, and where its lattice sits, comes from how the
  carries of f + P_i interact with the xor constant C. If that is predictable from the family alone, a pre-pass could aim at
  the right classes - the remaining route to real prediction.

## 7. The prediction search (2026-09-13, user's idea) - main\theory\research\phase_prepass.cu

- **phase_prepass**: per spot, all 15 grid positions, the kept families (top 5%) on a SAMPLE of K classes each, exact phase
  model; reports the best count found and how many (family, class) pairs reach 66/68/70. GPU, ~1 s per spot at K = 512.
- **Validation on 30 searched spots** (6 record spots + 24 spread over true best count 65-76):

  | pre-pass | corr with true best count | corr with best cq | time/spot |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2,048 random classes / family | 0.86 | 0.78 | ~3.7 s |
  | 512 random classes / family | 0.77 | 0.67 (families66: 0.75) | ~1 s |
  | 256 low-bit classes | 0.58 | 0.47 | ~0.5 s |
  | heat40 (same 30 spots) | 0.23 | 0.14 | - |

  Random samples work because a rich family's good classes form a lattice at family-specific high bits (section 6): a
  random sample of ~1/64 of the classes hits it often enough, a fixed low-bit sample misses most. Caveat: 30 spots, 6 of

- **Experiment**: pool = the next 1,000 fresh spots by heat (27.8-26.8) + 1,000 random fresh spots at heat 24-26 (fresh =

## 9. Master predictor: what a spot's geometry alone can say (2026-09-20)

**The exact algebra.** For family f, class c and chunk offset P_i:
`B_i(c) = (8M * ((H_i + c) ^ C') + L_i) mod 2^31`, with `H_i = ((f + P_i) >> 20) & 0x7FFF`,
`L_i = ((((f + P_i) mod 2^20) ^ K_lo) * M + 11) >> 17`, `C' = 0x5E43`, `M = 0x5DEECE66D`.
Two consequences: **the class enters ONLY through `(H_i + c) ^ C'`**, and **`H_i` can only be `(P_i >> 20)` or that
plus 1** (the carry out of the low 20 bits). So the SPOT fixes every pairwise H difference up to 1, whatever the seed;
the family chooses only which chunks are possible (parity of L) and the L values themselves.

**Three things that turned out NOT to be the mechanism** (research tools in `main\theory\research`):
- *Class-independent pairs* (`core_align`, `core_align2`). A pair whose H difference stays inside a run of C' keeps a
  constant B difference for ~15/16 of classes - but over 64 sampled classes NO pair agrees always (rich and control
  alike), and pairs agreeing in > 80% of classes: 0.0% in both. The transparent steps explain the good-class LATTICE
  (section 8), not the jackpot.
- *H structure of the aligned set*. Among the chunks actually slime together at a family's best class, H differences
  divisible by 4 occur 24.9% of the time - exactly chance, and identical to a random subset of the same size (25.0%).
- *H clustering of the possible set* (`hstruct`). Rich 1.09 vs control 0.91 at |dH| <= 8, nothing at 64 and above.
- *Possible count*. Controlled for: at equal possible chunks rich families average 49.6 vs 39.3 (bucket 140-144), and
  they sit **+8.5 counts above the control regression line** `mean = 6.93 + 0.229 * possible`. Richness is real and
  family-level, it lives in the L values, and it is visible in the AVERAGE class - which is what the probe exploits.

**What the geometry DOES predict** (`geo_predict` on all 1,724 searched spots, `geo_rank.exe` for production):
`hpair` = pairs of window chunks whose H values are within 512, minimum over the 15 grid positions - 222 offsets per
position, no family and no class, ~2,000 operations (the family cut costs 2^18 * 222).

| statistic | corr with the spot's exact best |
|---|---|
| hpair (H within 512, min over positions) | **-0.288** |
| H bins of 512 occupied (max over positions) | +0.262 |
| pairs with close low-20 bits | +0.02 |
| no-xor coherence (best 2^17 arc window of P*M) | -0.00 |
| heat40 (for comparison) | 0.10-0.23 |

It is nearly independent of heat (corr +0.15) and survives inside a narrow heat band (26-30: the low-hpair half holds
72+ at 21.0% and 74+ at 5.0%, the high half 12.5% and 2.0%). By quintile over all searched spots:

| hpair quintile (low = H spread out) | mean best | 72+ | 74+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-3 | 70.2-70.3 | 25-27% | 6-7% |
| 4 | 69.4 | 15.1% | 2.0% |
| 5 | 68.7 | 8.4% | 1.2% |

So it is a **free veto**: the worst 40% of spots hold a 74+ three to six times less often. `predict_cycle.ps1` now
picks half of every chunk by it (band `geo`, cached in `main\theory\prediction\universe_geo.csv`) and keeps the random
half as a control band, so the cycle lines themselves measure whether it holds up.
Distance from the world origin explains none of it (corr(best, |X|) = -0.00, corr(best, |Z|) = -0.04).

**Where this points.** A spot statistic this cheap is what a whole-world sweep would need (the heat survey costs
2^18 * 222 per spot; this costs 2,000). It is not yet a master predictor - r = -0.29 is a prior, not an answer - but
combined with the probe screen (section 8, 84-94% accurate at 0.24 s) the chain is now: geometry prior (free) ->
probe screen (sub-second) -> exact search (12.8 s) -> in-game measurement.

### 9a. Groundwork for the sweep (2026-09-20): the prior saturates, and there is no cheap middle ground

- **More geometry features do not help** (`geo_fit.cpp`, 21 features: hpair at five widths x min/mean/max, H bins,
  nearest-neighbour gaps, the same on the low 20 bits). Trained on 11,920 re-screened spots (screen labels), tested on
  the 1,724 spots with TRUE exact bests: holdout corr **+0.290**, against **-0.288** for `hpair512min` alone. The
  signal is one-dimensional - H clustering - and the ridge fit finds nothing else. Useful anyway as a filter: top
  decile of the fitted prior holds 74+ at **9.3%**, the rest 4.1%, the bottom decile **0.6%**.
- **A tiny seed sample does NOT rank spots** (`spot_probe.cpp`): N random (family, class) draws per spot, scored by
  their max or the mean of their top 8, against the true exact best of 1,724 spots -

  | draws | ops/spot | corr (max) | corr (mean of top 8) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 32 | 7.1e3 | +0.02 | +0.03 |
  | 128 | 2.8e4 | +0.03 | +0.03 |
  | 512 | 1.1e5 | +0.06 | +0.05 |
  | 2,048 | 4.5e5 | +0.05 | +0.04 |

  Random families say nothing: a probe only becomes meaningful AFTER the family cut (section 8), and the family cut is
  the 2^18 * 222 pass that heat also pays for. **So there is no cheap middle ground between geometry (2e3 ops, -0.29)
  and the family cut (5.8e7 ops).** Any world sweep must be: geometry prior everywhere -> family cut + probe screen on
  the survivors -> exact search on the best.

### 9b. CORRECTION (2026-09-20): the geometry prior measured on an UNSELECTED population

Section 9's numbers (corr -0.288, the worst 40% of spots holding a 74+ at 1-2% against 6-7%) were measured on the
1,724 **exact-searched** spots. Those spots are not a sample of anything: they were picked by heat and by the old
screen. Re-measured on the 11,920 spots of the re-screen (`heat\rescreen.ps1`, the general population of the
heat-20 universe, labelled by the probe screen):

| prior on 11,920 unselected spots | corr with the best count | top decile: 70+ | 72+ | 74+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| geometry (hpair512min) | **-0.150** | 12.6% | 2.1% | 0.25% |
| heat40 | +0.144 | 15.2% | 1.5% | 0.07% |
| **combined: heat z - geo z** | **+0.234** | **18.0%** | **2.7%** | 0.17% |
| base rate (all 11,920) | - | 12.9% | 1.8% | 0.14% |

So: geometry is about as strong as heat, not twice as strong, and the two are near-independent - combining them beats
either, which is what `predict_cycle` now uses for band A. But the honest limit is the last column: **no prior
enriches the TOP tier**. A 74+ sits at 0.14% base and 0.17% in the best decile. Priors move the middle of the
distribution (70+: 12.9% -> 18.0%); records come from the screen, not from the prior.
Consequence for the sweep: a geometry prior cannot replace screening on new territory, it can only order it. The
open question is now whether never-surveyed spots (below heat 20, or never sampled) hold 72+/74+ at a rate near the
heat-20 universe's 1.8% / 0.14% - that is one 8-minute screening run of 2,000 random world spots, and it decides
whether sweeping new territory is worth GPU hours at all.

### 9c. The world test (2026-09-20): heat is a population filter, and it cannot be skipped

3,000 RANDOM world spots (never surveyed, no heat filter) screened with the same stage-1 probe settings as the
re-screen, against the 11,920 spots of the heat-20 universe:

| population | mean best | 70+ | 72+ | 74+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heat-20 universe (n=11,920) | 67.8 | 12.9% | 1.8% | 0.14% |
| **random world (n=3,000)** | **60.7** | **0.00%** | **0.00%** | **0.00%** |

Not one of 3,000 random spots reached even 70 chunks. So heat is weak WITHIN the universe (corr +0.144, section 9b)
and decisive BETWEEN populations: the heat >= 20 cut is what makes a spot viable at all, and no cheap statistic
replaces it - geometry ranks inside a population, it cannot identify one (and random seed sampling ranks nothing,
section 9a). **A world sweep therefore needs the heat survey EXTENDED, not bypassed.** The 3,000,000-spot survey is
an asset, not a limitation; the 183,536 spots above heat 20 are the hunting ground we actually have.

## 10. The objective was wrong: score -39 BLOCKS, not chunk count (2026-09-20)

The search has always ranked layouts and spots by how many of the 222 chunks are slime. The number that matters is
what `/perimeterinfo` reads at the the player's AFK spot (feet at y -39 over the -64 floor): blocks = SUM over slime chunks
of that cell's spawn columns, `0 <= a^2 + b^2 <= 15808` (dy = 24, no inner no-spawn circle; a full floor = 49,669,
exactly the in-game number). Those are different things:

- Within ONE chunk count the blocks span ~2,500 (count 74: 15,517-18,006), while two extra chunks are worth ~580.
- At **23% of 2,335 searched spots** the best-blocks layout is not the best-count layout; 6% of spots lose over 500
  blocks that way (~2 cq, a 70 against a 72) and 29 spots over 1,000.
- Against the TRUE -39 number of 303 filed farms:

  | ranking objective | corr with the -39 count | best -39 in its top 20 |
  |---|---|---|
  | chunk count | **+0.21** | 17,984 |
  | cq blocks (inner 24, outer 128) | +0.37 | 18,407 |
  | **-39 blocks (`--y39`)** | **+0.74** | **18,407** (the true best) |

  Ranking by chunk count misses the best farm of the set in its top 20; ranking by -39 blocks finds it. The gap from
  1.0 is `afk_rescan`'s +-48-block AFK optimisation, which a fixed-position weighting cannot see.

**The phase model carries the weights for free**: a chunk is slime when w == phase (mod 5), so the sweep that counts
aligned chunks can add their weights instead - same cost. Verified against brute force over all 8,192 top values:
**2,000 checks, 0 mismatches** (`main\theory\research\phase_blocks.cpp`).
**And the existing bound stays sound**: a cell is worth at most 256 blocks, so blocks <= 256 * count and the count
bound at T = 60 cannot prune anything worth 60 cq or more. The proven fast bound kernel is untouched; only the exact
scoring of survivors changes.
`phase_prepass` now takes `--blocks` (cq weighting) and `--y39` (the raised-AFK weighting); `predict_cycle` screens

## 12. The 19,103 record, and what found it (2026-09-20/21)

**New in-game record: 19,103** (seed 230337960196917, AFK 14432930,5926460, 72.03 cq, 78 chunks), beating
19,012 by 91. `afk_rescan` estimated it EXACTLY. Note the cq: at **72.03** it is *lower* than the 73.19 it
beats - ranking farms by cq would have buried it. It won on the -39 objective, which is the whole point of
sections 10-11.

**The chain that found it**, every link built the same day:

1. **The bound.** Sort the 220 non-zero -39 cell weights descending; a layout with `k` slime chunks reads at
   most the prefix sum. The top 78 weights are all 256, so 19,103 needs **75+ chunks arithmetically** and
   78+ realistically. This makes CHUNK COUNT the right thing to screen on, even though count correlates only
   +0.21 with the in-game number - it is not a predictor, it is a *bound*.
2. **The screen gate** (`phase_spots -GateCount`, `record_cycle -GateCount`). The probe screen costs 0.32 s
   against the exact search's 14 s and **never over-states** a spot's best count (0 of 400). On the 18+54
   candidates exact-searched since, it has called the count **exactly** every time.
3. **New territory.** The gate over the old measured list found **0 spots at 74+ in 3,410**. The same gate
   over `universe_h20` finds **0.44%** - and the record came from a spot at heat 24.4 that the old
   top-10,000-X x top-300-lines cross product never contained.

**Stage 2** (`heat\candidates.ps1`): re-screen the 0.44% that pass with `--y39` before searching them.
Against the 18 candidates' real -39 numbers, screen blocks39 correlates **0.838** vs 0.506 for count. The
screen is a LOWER BOUND, not a prediction - it read 17,747 for the record spot whose farm is 19,103.

**Efficiency, and why count is self-defeating past a point.** Over 18 exact-searched candidates,
`peri / (256 x count)` ran 86.4% to 96.9%, median 91.3%; the record sits at 95.7%. Count and efficiency run
AGAINST each other - the 77-chunk spot was the worst at 88.4%, a 74 the best at 96.9% - because packing more
slime chunks into 226 cells forces them onto low-weight rim cells. To beat 19,103 needs 96.9% at 77 chunks
(the maximum ever seen), 95.7% at 78, 94.5% at 79, 93.3% at 80. The peri-optimal count is ~78-80, not maximal.

**Families do not recur across the world (negative, 9,545 spots).** Best-families repeat 1,215 times, which
looked like a 23x excess over uniform - but **1,171 of those pairs are spots within 2,048 blocks**, sharing
most of their 226-chunk window. Genuinely distant repeats: **44 observed against 52.9 expected by chance** -
below chance. A rich family is a local accident of the high bits of (seed + P), confirming the 2026-09-13
sister-spot test on 270x more spots. There is no transportable family.

**Where the tail stands.** 9,545 distinct universe spots: 74+ 0.42%, 76+ 0.063%, **78+ one spot - the
record's own**. Universe estimates ~769 / ~115 / ~19. The 74+ rate is firm across three batches; every 78+
number rests on a single observation (Poisson 95% CI on n=1: ~0.5 to 105), so treat ~19 as an order of
magnitude, not a count.

## 13. The 20,000 question, and what the archive already knew (2026-09-21)

### 13a. 20,000 is out of reach in this universe

A chunk fully inside the -39 disc contributes 256 blocks and no chunk contributes more, so the whole objective
is `-39 <= 256 x k` where k is the slime-chunk count AT THE FINAL AFK POINT. The ladder is flat - the 91st
largest cell weight is still 256 - so the bound is simply 256k:

| target | chunks required | efficiency needed at that k |
|---|---|---|
| 19,103 (the record) | 75 | 99.5% |
| **20,000** | **79** | **98.9%** |
| 20,000 at the record's realized 95.7% | **82** | - |

Supply, from 12,587 distinct screened spots (after the NUL/shape cleanup of 13d) - **each extra chunk is
2.61x rarer**:

| k | observed | 1 in | expected in all 183,536 spots |
|---|---|---|---|
| 76 | 8 | 1,573 | - |
| 77 | 2 | 6,293 | - |
| 78 | 1 (the record) | 12,587 | ~15 |
| 79 | 0 | - | ~6 |
| 80 | 0 | - | ~2.3 |
| 82 | 0 | - | **~0.3** |

So the universe probably contains no spot capable of 20,000, and confirming that costs a full 12.7-hour
screen. **The realistic ceiling here is ~19,300-19,500.** 20,000 needs a larger universe, not a better search.

### 13b. The count bound is on the FINAL count, not the screen's count

Over 536 searched spots joined to their filed farms' -39 numbers, the best -39 achieved per exact BestCount:

| exact BestCount | best -39 achieved | as % of 256 x count |
|---|---|---|
| 70 | 17,996 | **100.4%** |
| 74 | 19,018 | **100.4%** |
| 76 | 18,970 | 97.5% |
| 78 | 19,103 | 95.7% |

Two entries exceed 100% of their own ceiling, which is only possible if refinement moves the AFK point to a
window containing MORE slime chunks than the spot's chunk-aligned best. It does. So section 11-12's rule
("beating 19,103 needs 78+ chunks") is wrong as stated: it binds the final configuration, and the screen's
count is a weak proxy for that. Among filed farms, **corr(count, final -39) = -0.08**, and corr(pre-refine
`blocks39`, final -39) is only **0.278** - because the refinement uplift is mean **+1,019 blocks with sd
737**, which is larger than the entire spread of the top 20 farms (19,103 down to 18,690 = 413).
**The refinement step contributes more variance to the final number than any pre-refinement ranking explains.**

### 13c. The archive, ranked by the objective for the first time

`afk_rescan` over all 2,902 filed 66+ farms took 45 seconds; the ranking is `main\records\y39_all.csv`.
Only 401 of them had ever been given a -39 number (`over66_y-39\` skips farms whose record image is missing).

- **No record was hiding in the archive** - 19,103 is genuinely #1 of 2,902.
- **11 of the top 50 by -39 are outside the top 50 by cq.** At cq 69.x the -39 range is **17,289-18,598**,
  a spread of 1,309 blocks, against ~265 blocks per cq point. cq ordering is not -39 ordering, and the
  66-cq filing gate is close to random with respect to the real objective.
- The record spot is **exhausted**: all 11,634 of its layouts through `afk_rescan` return 19,103, from
  several different seeds converging on the same AFK point (14432930, 5926460).
- **The AFK window is not binding**: windows 48 / 96 / 160 over the top 60 farms give identical maxima and
  identical means. The refined AFK point is a genuine local peak; widening the search finds nothing.

### 13e. `possible` does not rank within the searched range

Over 198,513 layouts from 300 spots, corr(`possible`, `count`) = **-0.005**, and max count per `possible`
is flat at 74-76 from possible 143 to 164 once sample size is controlled - while `max/possible` falls
steadily from 0.53 at 134 to 0.39 at 178. More possible chunks do not buy a higher count; the phase split
just divides them more thinly. This does not condemn the family cut, because `phase_spots.ps1` already runs
the CENTRE position at `--phase-top-pct 100` (the cut applies only to the offset grid) - but it does remove
`possible` as a candidate ranking statistic.

### 13f. The ceiling, three ways - and the extrapolation that lies

Per-spot best -39 over 556 exact-searched spots (median 17,224, 90th 17,887, **max 19,103**). Fitting the
upper tail and extrapolating to more spots:

| method | ceiling it implies |
|---|---|
| exponential tail on the top 10% of exceedances | 20,044 at 5,000 spots; **21,298** at the full universe |
| chunk ladder + the 2.61x-per-chunk count tail (13a) | ~19,300-19,500 |
| **GPD, method of moments, highest usable threshold** | **upper endpoint 19,744** |

**The exponential fit is wrong and says so itself**: at n=536 it predicts a max of 19,268 when the observed
max at n=556 is 19,103. It over-predicts at the sample size where we can check it, because an exponential
tail is unbounded and this objective is not - `-39 <= 256k` caps it. Fitted GPD shape is negative at every
threshold (xi = -0.13 to -0.25) and the fitted endpoint FALLS as the threshold rises (20,774 -> 20,161 ->
19,744), which is what a tail lighter than exponential looks like from below.

**Take ~19,400-19,750 as the ceiling of this universe.** 20,000 sits at or just past the fitted endpoint:
not provably impossible, but past the point where any honest extrapolation supports it. This revises 13a's
~19,300-19,500 slightly upward. Any future estimate built on an unbounded tail should be discarded on sight -
it is the same failure mode as the heat bands, the family "23x excess" and the duplicate-inflated 78 count.
