# watchlist\ - the spot archive (protocol, user 2026-10-03)

`watchlist.csv` is a permanent archive of SPOTS worth remembering, not farms. It exists because the top of the
record archive is made of a handful of exceptional spots, not exceptional seeds: the top 50 farms at y -39 come
from only 15 distinct spots, 45 of the 50 from spots that produced two or more of them, and 10 of the top 50 from
the record spot alone (9060800,-15431040). A spot that produces one top farm nearly always produces several.

## Columns

`spot_x, spot_z` - the spot (screen coordinates) | `max_chunks` - highest chunk count among its filed farms
`best_b39` / `best_cq` - its best farm | `farms_filed` - how many 66+ farms it has produced
`found` - which section found it | `reason` - why it is listed | `deep_searched` - date, blank if never
`note` - what a deep search found

## Entry rule

A spot is archived when it reaches **79+ chunks** in a filed farm. That is the measured gate: all three 19,000+
farms ever found came from 79+ spots, and none of the 925 spots that topped out at 74-78 has produced one, despite
being ~75% of all exact-search time. Spots producing 2+ farms in the archive top 100 are also worth adding.

## Reporting protocol (standing, user 2026-10-03)

After every section, the results report lists any NEW spots that cleared the gate and **asks whether to scan them
deeper**. Nothing is re-scanned without being asked. Deep scans are logged back into `deep_searched` / `note` so a
spot is never deep-searched twice.

## The deep-search test (2026-10-03) - CLOSED, and NOT part of any run

A one-off experiment, run by hand with explicit flags. **It is not the default and must not become one:**
`heat\phase_spots.ps1` keeps its shipped budget (`-TopPct 5`, refine 400/200/200, `-TopPositions 10`), and a
section passes no search flags at all. The experiment used ~9x that budget - 25% of families, refine 1500/800/800,
all 15 AFK positions, 2,100-2,800 layouts refined instead of ~600 - at 41-47 s a spot against ~4.7 s.

**Result: 8 of 8 spots unchanged. Zero new farms, zero improvement to any spot's best.**

| spot | best before | best after 9x search |
|---|---|---|
| 14432976,5926432 | 19,103 | 19,103 |
| -15986784,-22325440 | 18,692 | 18,692 |
| -29388896,19617584 | 18,507 | 18,507 |
| -13677024,-22325440 | 17,969 | 17,969 |
| -509360,19617600 | 17,709 | 17,709 |
| -25130288,16457136 | 17,632 | 17,632 |
| -7531104,20099648 | 17,386 | 17,386 |
| 9060800,-15431040 (record) | 19,734 | 19,734 |

So the standard exact-search budget already SATURATES a spot - there are no blocks left on the table at spots we
have visited. This empirically refutes the 2026-10-01 audit's `exact-budget` hypothesis ("did we leave blocks at
spots already searched?"), which had never been verified. Do not re-propose deeper per-spot searching as a way to
find more; the remaining value is in visiting NEW spots.

CAVEAT on the gate: `max_chunks` here is the chunk count of a FILED farm (post exact search). The screen's own
count (the 74+ promote threshold) is a different, correlated quantity - the deep runs above reported 74-78 for
spots whose filed farms show 80-81. Do not turn "79+ chunks" into a screen-side promote threshold without first
measuring the screen-count to exact-count mapping.
